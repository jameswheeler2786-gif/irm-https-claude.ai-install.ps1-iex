The role

This isn't a flyer-handing
ambassador job

We're looking for someone who can build a campus network — not someone who wants a few flexible hours.

What you'd actually do

  • Recruit recurring organisers: societies, clubs, chapters, sports teams, promoters
  • Help those hosts publish real events, not just hand out a link
  • Hold a founding position on your campus, with attribution in writing
  • Run your own events on Outly and keep normal host economics
  • Turn activated hosts into referrals for the next ones

What it isn't

  • Handing out flyers or free merch
  • Posting a referral code and hoping
  • Paid by the hour for activity nobody measures
  • Chasing app downloads as the goal
  • A job you can do without knowing anyone

The first 30 days

You'll know if it's working
within a month

This is the plan we'd hand you on day one. Success is measured in activated hosts, not downloads.

Days 1–3

Map the campus

Build a list of 100 high-value nodes: societies, chapters, club sports, promoters, halls and recurring organisers. Rank them by how often they run things.

Days 4–10

Land the first hosts

Personally contact your top 30. Aim for five live host accounts — and help them publish their first event rather than leaving them with a link.

Days 11–20

Make it visible

Get a cluster of events live close together, so people see Outly repeatedly through society channels, group chats and host audiences.

Days 21–30

Let it compound

Ask every activated host who else runs events. Target their collaborators. The goal is a host network that starts recruiting itself.

A target, not a quota. We aim for around 10 activated recurring hosts in the first 30 days — it's how we measure whether the approach is working, not a number you're contractually held to. Nobody has been penalised for a slow first month.

FAQ

Common questions

How much time does it take?

It's flexible and you set your own hours — but be realistic. The first month is the heavy one, because that's when you're mapping your campus and landing your first hosts. After that it's largely relationship maintenance and referrals.

How exactly do I get paid?

The proposed model pays you a share of qualifying ticket sales from hosts and organisations you personally introduce. Attribution is direct — you to the host — and for societies or chapters it attaches to the organisation, so it survives a committee change. Exact rates and the full terms are confirmed in writing before you start.

Do I need experience?

Not formal experience. What helps is having done something adjacent: sold tickets, run a society social, organised an event, grown a page or community, or sold anything on commission. Access and initiative matter far more than a polished CV.

Can I still run my own events?

Yes — and we'd encourage it. You get standard host economics on your own events on top of the partner role, and running events yourself makes you much better at recruiting other hosts.

How many partners per campus?

One or two. This is deliberately not a big cohort — we'd rather have a small number of well-supported people with a real territory than hundreds of names on a list.

What happens after I apply?

If your answers show real campus access, we'll set up a short call to talk through your network and how you'd approach the first 30 days. If it's a fit, you get written terms and an onboarding session before you start.
